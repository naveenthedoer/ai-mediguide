Title: Understanding Your Medications — Patient Guide
Source: Based on NHS Inform, MedlinePlus patient information

BLOOD PRESSURE MEDICINES:

Ramipril / Lisinopril (ACE inhibitors):
- What they do: relax blood vessels, protect kidneys
- Take: once daily, with or without food
- Common side effects: dry cough (common — tell your doctor), dizziness when standing
- Important: do not take if pregnant; avoid NSAIDs like ibuprofen
- Tell your doctor if: you develop swelling of lips/face/throat (angioedema — urgent)

Amlodipine (calcium channel blocker):
- What it does: relaxes blood vessel walls, lowers blood pressure
- Take: once daily, same time each day
- Common side effects: ankle swelling, flushing, headache (usually settle after 2 weeks)
- Important: do not stop suddenly without talking to your doctor

Bisoprolol (beta-blocker):
- What it does: slows heart rate, reduces the heart's workload
- Take: once daily in the morning
- Side effects: tiredness, cold hands/feet, vivid dreams
- Important: never stop suddenly — taper under medical supervision
- Tell your doctor if: you have asthma or breathing problems before starting

DIABETES MEDICINES:

Metformin:
- What it does: reduces sugar production in the liver
- Take: with meals to reduce stomach upset; start with low dose
- Side effects: nausea, diarrhoea (usually improve after 2-4 weeks)
- Important: stop if vomiting or unable to eat/drink (sick day rule)
- Tell your doctor before: CT scans with dye, any surgery

Empagliflozin / Dapagliflozin (SGLT2 inhibitors):
- What they do: help kidneys remove excess sugar in urine
- Benefits: also protect heart and kidneys
- Side effects: genital thrush (keep area clean and dry), increased urination
- Important: stop before surgery; drink plenty of water; watch for signs of DKA

Semaglutide / Dulaglutide (GLP-1 injections):
- What they do: stimulate insulin release, reduce appetite
- Take: once weekly injection (pen device)
- Side effects: nausea, vomiting (usually settles); inject in tummy, thigh, or upper arm
- Benefits: significant weight loss (3-5kg average)

WHEN TO SEEK URGENT HELP:
- Chest pain or pressure → call 999
- Sudden severe headache → call 999
- Blood sugar very low (<4 mmol/L) and not improving with sugar → call 999
- Swelling of face, lips or throat after starting new medicine → call 999
- Severe stomach pain with vomiting on SGLT2 inhibitor → call 999 (possible DKA)
